EB-1A AAO Decisions Explorer

Every published EB-1A ("extraordinary ability") appeal and motion decision from the USCIS Administrative Appeals Office, aggregated so you can see what actually happens — and why.

What is this?

When USCIS denies an EB-1A petition (the employment-based, first-preference immigrant category for people of "extraordinary ability"), the petitioner can appeal to the Administrative Appeals Office (AAO) or file a motion to reopen or reconsider. The AAO publishes its decisions — with names removed — in a public reading room. This page aggregates those published EB-1A decisions.

These are non-precedent decisions: each one binds only the case it decides. They don't set policy, but in bulk they show how the AAO actually applies the ten regulatory criteria and the "final merits" test.

Why the numbers look grim — read this before you panic. Every case here was already denied once and then appealed. Petitions that are approved the first time never reach the AAO and never show up in this data. So a low sustain rate below says "appeals of denials rarely succeed" — it says nothing about your odds of being approved on a first filing, which are far better. This is a classic selection-biased sample; treat it as a map of where denied cases went wrong, not as your personal probability.

This is not legal advice.This is an automated statistical summary of public documents. It may contain extraction errors, and it cannot evaluate your case. Talk to a qualified immigration attorney before making decisions.

    Methodology & data quality

    Where the data comes from. Decision PDFs are downloaded from the USCIS AAO public decisions repository (the "B2 — Aliens with Extraordinary Ability" collection), then text is extracted from each PDF and parsed with automated pattern matching. The scraper and parser live in the uscis_eb1a_scraper/ directory of this project's GitHub repository.

    Automated extraction is imperfect. Outcomes, case types, and criteria determinations are pulled from decision text by regular expressions, not by a human reading each case. Scanned or oddly formatted PDFs can defeat the parser. Criterion "met" determinations are tri-state — met, not met, or not addressed/unclear — and the unclear ones are excluded from every rate on this page, as are cases whose overall outcome could not be determined.
